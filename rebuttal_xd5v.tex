\section*{Key Questions}

\textbf{Q1: Log traces.}
The two mechanisms have different interaction structures. The \textbf{LMSR
market} (Section~6) has no dialogue: each agent independently observes prices
and submits a trade. The \textbf{Semantic market} (Section~5) has a sequential
MM~$\leftrightarrow$~Trader exchange. Full JSONL traces for both are in
\texttt{traces/} of the \supp. In a representative LMSR episode, blind
malicious agents inadvertently support the correct outcome---illustrating
the imperfect adversarial effectiveness discussed below. We will revise
Figures~1--2 to clarify this distinction.

\textbf{Q2: Market population / mode collapse.}
Each experiment uses $N{=}5$ agents of the same model family with independent
calls and distinct role prompts. From our episode logs: $13.3\%$ of TruthfulQA
2v3 Blind episodes had all honest agents vote incorrectly with $100\%$ pairwise
agreement ($n{=}3$). Correlated errors are real but adversarial agents exhibit
the same correlation (bidirectional noise). Honest claim: the market provides
adversarial resistance, not Condorcet-style diversity. Full code released as
supplementary.

\textbf{Q3: Vote Acc $>0\%$ in 2v3 Informed.}
The theoretical prediction is $0\%$. The observed $0.3\%$ is sampling noise:
LLMs occasionally fail their adversarial role prompt---the same $p_\text{adv}
< 1.0$ property discussed below. $0.3\% \approx 0$; voting is essentially
entirely compromised. A clarifying note will be added to Section~6.2.1.

\section*{LMSR Choice and Economic Basis}

LMSR is the only \emph{strictly proper} scoring rule among the alternatives:
agents maximise expected utility by reporting true beliefs when resolution is
against ground truth. CLOB lacks liquidity guarantees for thin markets ($N{=}5$);
RFQ is bilateral and non-public; LS-LMSR trades properness for bounded loss.
A comparative paragraph will be added to Section~3.2, and Section~2.2 expanded
to orient economics-unfamiliar readers.

\section*{Section 6.3: Pump-and-Dump and the Epistemic Arbitrage Argument}

Section~6.3's argument assumes fixed-directional adversaries, not wealth-maximisers.
We concede this and will state it explicitly.

\textbf{Analytical intuition on bluffing.} A bluffing adversary bets truthfully
for $k$ rounds, then defects. LMSR payoffs are proportional to shares: malicious
hold $3/5$, honest $2/5$. After settlement both scale by $(1{+}r)$---the $3{:}2$
wealth ratio is preserved. Bluffing provides no relative capital advantage; the
genuine vulnerability requires persistent cross-episode state current LLMs lack.

\textbf{Empirical confirmation.} Ablation augmenting prompts with capital balances
(\supp, Section~5): accuracy dropped $12.2$pp ($73.3\%{\to}61.1\%$), malicious
wealth doubled ($33.3\%{\to}67.2\%$). Contribution~\#3 will be scoped to
fixed-directional adversaries; wealth-maximising adversaries flagged as future work.
